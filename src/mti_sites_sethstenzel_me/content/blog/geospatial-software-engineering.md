I think there probably is a time in many developers lives when they change perspective from job, to craftsman. As I sat at my desk last week, toiling in confusion with all the AI related content being exponentially pushed to ever feed I own, I had a moment of clarity. I realized, I'm a little tired of chasing this new software developer career I've been struggling to break into these last 4 years. I know the things I like about software, and instead of being just another name on a random development team of some company, a glancing data point in that years employee layoff calculation machine, I'm doing it my way. I'm going to target the career I want, and after looking over the ecosystems, technologies, and years of personal projects, I've decided Geospatial Software Engineer is what I will be.

The last few days I've conversed heavily with ChatGPT, and Claude, and I have a what I think is a fairly solid path forward. Unfortunately this path will realistically take 1.5 to 2 years. Both a long time, especially in this AI paced market, and not that long at all. Each day, week, month, year, I will target and build solid real world Geospatial Development skills.

The goal will be a skill set that lets me build systems that turn the unreliable real-world spatial data into correct, observable performant, products and decisions.

Strong focus will be given to python backend, integration, automation, and data-centered libraries. I'll also be integrating strong SQL, PostgreSQL, and PostGIS for deeper technical specialization. GIS knowledge such as vector, raster, geodesy, special analysis, data quality and domain reasoning will help set me apart. I will also add in a good amount of data engineering with parquet, arrow, Pollars, DuckDB, dbt, Dagster, Kafka, Contracts and Observability. Finally some front end polishing with TypeScript, React, and MapLibre. In addition to role specific topics, I will add solid foundations in Linux, Git, Docker, Terraform, network, security, and SRE for better production ownership skills.

The final test will be my ability, to take an ambiguous spatial-data problem through the complete engineering lifecycle.

## Weekly Operating System

Weekly rhythm:
| Day       | Default function                                                     |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Monday    | Learn the week’s concepts and define acceptance criteria             |
| Tuesday   | Closed-book drills and small controlled experiments                  |
| Wednesday | Implement the main production feature                                |
| Thursday  | Test failure modes, correctness, security and performance            |
| Friday    | Release, document, demonstrate and convert work into career evidence |

Every workday is exactly two focused hours:

- **15 minutes — Closed-book recall:** code, SQL, diagrams, definitions or failure modes without AI.
- **30 minutes — Targeted study:** one course section, official document, credible talk or focused reading.
- **65 minutes — Execution:** build, query, test, benchmark, deploy, debug or interview practice.
- **10 minutes — Close:** run quality checks, commit, log the lesson and write the next smallest action.

## Flagship Projects

I will also be focusing on two large projects and a capstone project:
### Flagship 1 — Geospatial Data Platform

A public-data system that ingests vector and raster sources, validates and versions them, stores operational data in PostGIS, publishes analytical snapshots, serves APIs and vector tiles, and renders an interactive map.

Required proof:

- Restartable ingestion and backfills
- CRS and geometry correctness
- SQL/PostGIS query plans and performance budgets
- Parquet/GeoParquet analytical outputs
- TypeScript/MapLibre interface
- CI/CD, monitoring, backup, recovery and runbook

### Flagship 2 — Real-World Decision Product

A narrowly scoped GIS/data product for agriculture, utilities, logistics, forestry, transportation, environmental work, public infrastructure or another domain where you can access a user or credible proxy.

Required proof:

- A specific user decision or workflow
- Measured reduction in labor, errors or uncertainty
- Explicit data ownership, limitations and risk
- Operational handoff and documentation

### Rust Component

A CLI, service or extension that fixes a measured performance, memory, concurrency or deployment problem. Rust is removed when total-system benchmarks do not justify its ongoing cost.

### Capstone

A final system combining the strongest elements of the previous work into one employer- and user-evaluable product. It must be narrow enough to finish and deep enough to demonstrate ownership.

## Career Campaign

The last focus of this initiative is a Career Campaign. While the process may take up to two years, I will be looking to gain a supplementing job that is at least adjacent to my targeted career path.

The plan is roughly:

- **Weeks 1–10:** sharpen positioning, clean GitHub and reconnect with former colleagues.
- **Weeks 10–20:** apply to 3–5 highly matched Python/GIS/data roles weekly.
- **Weeks 21–40:** publish substantial platform and product evidence; begin direct outreach.
- **Weeks 41–60:** pursue advanced backend/data roles; use Rust only where it improves role fit.
- **Weeks 61–70:** package consulting offers and conduct disciplined interview drills.
- **Weeks 71–80:** run an intensive targeted campaign, negotiate and choose the next specialization from actual market evidence.

I will also be documenting my progress here for my own record keeping, and in case someone else in the future looks for information on a similar career path.
